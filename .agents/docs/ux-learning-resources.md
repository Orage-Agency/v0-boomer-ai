# UX study resources for Boomer AI

Selected resources for designing a calm, accessible AI-learning experience for adults. Start with the videos in order, then use the Google PAIR chapters to apply human-centered AI patterns.

## Videos

1. [4 Things to Do When Designing for Older Users — Nielsen Norman Group](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=G0cRp7wFt3s)  
   A concise introduction to readability, interaction, and design considerations for older adults.

2. [User Testing with Older Adults — Nielsen Norman Group](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=09zwGWCLoLQ)  
   Practical guidance for planning and conducting usability sessions with older adult participants.

3. [Check Error Message Quality with a Scoring Rubric — Nielsen Norman Group](https://www.nngroup.com/videos/error-message-scoring-rubric/)  
   A short video on making system feedback visible, constructive, and respectful of user effort. Apply the constructive-feedback ideas; Boomer AI does not use scores or grades for learners.

## Read alongside the videos

- [Usability for Older Adults: Challenges and Changes — Nielsen Norman Group](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/usability-for-senior-citizens/) — Research on readability, input flexibility, and confusing error messages; avoid treating older adults as a uniform group.
- [Progressive Disclosure — Nielsen Norman Group](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/progressive-disclosure/) — How revealing advanced or secondary options when needed can keep an initial screen focused.
- [Errors + Graceful Failure — Google People + AI Guidebook](https://pair.withgoogle.com/guidebook-v2/chapter/errors-failing/) — Design recovery paths that support the user's relationship with AI.
- [Explainability + Trust — Google People + AI Guidebook](https://pair.withgoogle.com/guidebook-v2/chapter/explainability-trust/) — Explain what AI does and give users ways to correct unexpected behavior.
- [Feedback + Control — Google People + AI Guidebook](https://pair.withgoogle.com/guidebook-v2/chapter/feedback-controls/) — Keep feedback understandable and user control clear in AI experiences.

## Study prompts

After each resource, apply its ideas to one real Boomer AI flow:

- What is the user's goal, and what is the one primary next step?
- How does the interface let the person recover, revise, or ask for a different explanation?
- Does feedback teach without grading or embarrassing the person?
- Are learning and optional play visually distinct through hierarchy and labels rather than decorative color?
- What would you ask an intended user to do in a moderated usability session?

These materials provide starting points, not a substitute for testing with Boomer AI's own users.
